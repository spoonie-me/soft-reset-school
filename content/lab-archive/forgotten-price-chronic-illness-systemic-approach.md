Years of chasing survival, specialist to specialist, procedure to procedure, turned me into someone who could recite my own symptoms with clinical precision and had forgotten how to actually live.

Hobbies went first, replaced by the need to rest before the next appointment. Social life thinned out, spent on fatigue instead. Exercise, which helps long-term, got dropped because the short-term discomfort felt like too much to spend. Even food became about what was easiest rather than what actually helped, and the body paid for it later. Purpose, the part of life that exists outside the illness, faded into the background of just getting through the day.

That's the survival trap, and it's real. It's also not the only option. Reclaiming some baseline of wellbeing has to be a priority alongside survival, not something deferred until the illness is "handled," because for a lot of us it never fully is.

That means treating health as more than a symptom list: mental, emotional, and social wellbeing count too, and joy isn't a luxury item. It means treating food, movement, and sleep as the actual foundation they are, adjusted to what your body can currently do, not what it used to do. It means holding onto something outside the diagnosis, a passion, a contribution, anything that isn't organized entirely around illness management. And it means treating your own body as a partner in this rather than an adversary to manage.

For anyone bedridden or housebound, this still applies, just at a different scale: nutritional guidance and hydration when intake is limited, gentle movement within whatever range is actually available, a consistent sleep routine, low-effort ways to stay mentally engaged, and staying connected to people even when it's only a call or a text. None of it is a cure. All of it is the difference between surviving a day and being present for part of it.

This isn't easy, and it isn't a solo project either. But the alternative, treating wellness as something to get to later, has never actually arrived for the people who tried that path. It has to be part of the plan now.
