# Outreach drafts: pilot learners and employers

*Drafts for Roi to edit and send. Nothing here has been sent. Keep every claim checkable against
`docs/VISION.md`: no job, income or outcome promises, and a certificate shows a passed exam.*

The point of both groups is to learn, not to sell. Ten honest conversations will tell you more
than any more building. Write down what people say in their own words.

## 1. Pilot learners (5 to 10 people returning to work)

**Who:** people going back to work after illness, burnout, caring or a break, who might use one
specialisation. Look in the Spooniversity community first (you already have their trust), then
your LinkedIn network, then a few ME/CFS, long-COVID and carers' groups where you are a member.

**Message (short, personal, change the first line to fit the person):**

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm building Soft Reset School: short, low-energy lessons for people going back to work at a
> pace they can sustain. It's early, and I'd like a few real people to try it and tell me
> honestly what works and what doesn't.
>
> If you're up for it, I'll give you free access to one track (your pick), including the exam and
> certificate. In return I'd ask for about three lessons' worth of your time and a 20-minute
> chat, or five written questions if talking is too much. You can stop at any point, no reason
> needed, and I won't use anything you say without asking you first.
>
> Nothing here promises a job. It's practice and a certificate that shows you passed the exam.
> I just want to know if it's useful.
>
> Would you like to try it?
> Roi

**What you give them:** a hardship code from `npm run hardship:code` (run in `backend/`), one per
person. They redeem it on the Pricing page.

**What to ask afterwards (pick what fits, never all):**
1. What were you hoping this would help you do?
2. Where did you get stuck, bored or tired?
3. Was there a lesson you'd have skipped? Was any one really good?
4. Did the lower-energy path help, or get in the way?
5. Would you pay €99 for it? What would make that an easy yes, and what would make it a no?
6. Who would you show the certificate to? What would they say?

## 2. Employers and agencies (accessibility track)

**Who:** web and digital agencies serving EU public sector or e-commerce clients, accessibility
consultancies, in-house digital or compliance leads at larger EU retailers, banks and transport
companies, and vocational-rehab or disability-employment organisations. The European
Accessibility Act has applied to many consumer-facing digital services since 28 June 2025, so
this is a live question for them.

**First email (a conversation, not a pitch):**

> Subject: 15 minutes on accessibility testing and hiring?
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Roi Shternin-Martini, a trainer in Vienna. I'm building a training programme where people
> who use assistive technology every day learn to audit digital products for accessibility, and
> I want to check it against what employers actually need.
>
> I'm not selling anything in this email. Could I ask you 15 minutes of questions about how you
> find and trust accessibility testing today? Whatever you say stays between us unless you tell
> me otherwise.
>
> If it's useful, I'm also happy to look at one of your pages and send you a short, honest note on
> what a user of a screen reader would run into. No charge.
>
> Roi

**Follow-up after a week, once:**

> Hi [name], a quick nudge on my note below. No problem at all if it's not a good moment. If there
> is someone better placed to ask, I'd be glad of an introduction. Roi

**Questions for the call:**
1. Who owns accessibility testing today, and how do you find them?
2. What do you do when you need an audit? Who do you buy it from, and what does it cost?
3. What makes you trust one tester or report over another?
4. Would a certificate from a small school mean anything to you? What would make it count?
5. Would you hire or contract someone who has the certificate and a portfolio? What else would
   you need to see?
6. If I delivered audits myself first, what would you want in a report?

**The one thing to find out:** does anyone pay for this, and what would make them trust a new
tester? The answer decides whether the accessibility track is the one to deepen.

## 3. What to record after every conversation

Keep it in a simple table. One row per person, written within the hour.

| Date | Who (role, type) | Pilot or employer | What they said, in their words | What surprised me | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

After ten rows, read them together and write three sentences: what people want, what they would
pay for, and what to build next.

## 4. Rules

- Ask before quoting anyone or naming them anywhere.
- Never promise a job, an income, or a result. Say "practice and a certificate".
- If someone mentions a health condition, you did not ask. Don't note it unless they want you to.
- Don't send follow-ups beyond one. Silence is an answer.
