A study in the *British Journal of Health Psychology* looked at post-traumatic growth, the positive psychological change that can follow a genuinely difficult experience, in doctors after the COVID-19 pandemic. The doctors who experienced PTG were more likely to feel empowered to help patients, more satisfied with their work, and less likely to burn out. [The study is here](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjhp.12642).

What's worth sitting with is the direction of the effect. Growth after hardship didn't make these doctors harder or more detached. It made them more capable of empathy, not less. The same pattern shows up in patients: the ones who find some form of meaning inside a diagnosis often manage their condition with more consistency, not because the illness got easier, but because they stopped fighting the fact of it.

None of that makes the original hardship worth it. It just means the hardship isn't only loss.
