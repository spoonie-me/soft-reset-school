**Medical gaslighting and the empathy it costs**

A New York Times piece on medical gaslighting is worth your time: [read it here](https://www.nytimes.com/2022/07/29/well/mind/medical-gaslighting.html). It describes what happens when a provider dismisses a patient's symptoms until the patient starts doubting their own perception of their own body.

I wrote about the same thing in *Revolution from My Bed*:

> "We didn't wake up deciding to be rebellious, sick, or uncooperative in our healing, and they didn't choose to be unkind."

Neither side chose the dynamic. That doesn't make it harmless.

**What wearables actually change**

[Digital Health Wire covered](https://digitalhealthwire.com/can-wearables-help-measure-patient-outcomes/) how wearables are starting to measure patient outcomes directly, not just vital signs. Tracked well, that data gives patients a way to see their own patterns and gives clinicians something better than a self-report from memory. The interesting shift isn't the device. It's who gets to see the data first.

**Patients on stage, not just in the room**

Conferences that put patients up as speakers, not just as the subject of the talk, get a different conversation. It's a small structural choice with a large effect on what a room is willing to hear.
