Healthcare has this backward. A doctor's time is treated as scarce and precious. A patient's time is treated as free.

Patients wait weeks for an appointment, hours in the waiting room once they arrive, and get minutes with the doctor once they're finally seen. Meanwhile clinicians are run through tightly packed schedules, one patient after another, with no room to breathe either.

Clinical expertise deserves respect. So does the time a patient spent arranging childcare, taking off work, and travelling to be there. The current arrangement devalues one side's investment while crushing the other side under an impossible schedule. Rushed visits leave no room for real questions. Patients can't absorb what's dumped on them in a compressed few minutes, and the pressure itself breeds mistrust.

Prioritizing the doctor's time above all else is a relic of a model where the patient was expected to be passive. Today's patients expect a partnership, and a partnership means both sides' time counts.

That requires taking pressure off the clinician too: reasonable schedules, team-based care, and virtual visits where they genuinely help. It means designing care around when patients actually need it: evening hours, fast access to simple answers, less friction for basic questions that don't need an appointment at all.

Mostly it means treating the relationship as the priority, not the throughput. Valuing patient time the way we already claim to value clinician time produces more engaged patients and less burnt-out providers. That's not a soft goal. It's the whole point of the system.
