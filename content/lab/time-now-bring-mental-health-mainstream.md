Mental healthcare should be as easy to access as seeing a doctor for a physical symptom. It still isn't, and the gap between those two experiences is where a lot of preventable suffering happens.

Parity laws exist on paper. Insurers still find loopholes that restrict mental health coverage in ways they wouldn't get away with for a physical diagnosis. Real parity means no exceptions, not a policy that reads well and covers less.

The provider shortage is real and it's structural: entire regions are formally designated mental health professional shortage areas. Growing that workforce requires actually retaining people in it, which means reasonable workloads and pay, not just more training pipelines feeding into unsustainable jobs.

Access also means proximity: care available locally, through telehealth, through mobile crisis response, not a multi-hour drive or a months-long waitlist standing between a person and help they need now. Schools and workplaces have a role too: counselors, social workers, and mental health benefits built in as infrastructure, not a perk.

None of this is abstract. Stigma keeps people quiet, and quiet is where the worst outcomes happen. Making mental healthcare as mainstream and accessible as physical care isn't a lofty goal. It's overdue maintenance on a system that's been treating half of health as optional for too long.
