Patients feel so detached from the medical process happening around them that they end up reaching for any bit of control they can get. What doctors dismiss as "cyberchondria," patients reading up on their own condition, isn't really a trust problem. It's a response to feeling shut out of a process that's happening to their own body.

The real gap is contact between visits. The technology to fix it exists. It's rarely deployed.

Most clinic visits already feel impersonal: a doctor typing more than making eye contact, a hand-off between nurse after nurse, then a rushed few minutes before the next patient. By the time the appointment ends, there's a list of things you meant to ask and didn't get to.

So patients go home and search. Some of what comes back contradicts what the doctor said, which produces more anxiety, not less. Calling this an epidemic misses the actual mechanism: it's not that patients trust Google over their doctor. It's that they want more time, more contact, more say in their own treatment plan, and searching is what's available when none of that is on offer.

Video visits and secure messaging between appointments already exist as tools. Most doctors still don't offer them, despite the obvious benefit. Patients also want direct access to their own results and notes, because understanding your own data is not a fringe request, it's the baseline for actually participating in your own care.

None of this replaces expert guidance, and unfiltered searching can send anxiety somewhere unhelpful fast. The fix isn't telling patients to stop Googling. It's a doctor taking the extra few minutes to explain a result clearly enough that searching for the answer afterward isn't necessary. A short check-in message on how a new treatment is landing does more for trust than most people expect.

Patients already know doctors are working inside a broken system. The ask isn't more resources from an already stretched clinician. It's not being treated like a file number when the something-more costs almost nothing to give.
