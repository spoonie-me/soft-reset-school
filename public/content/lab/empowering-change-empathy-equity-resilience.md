**Empathy is a skill, not a personality trait**

An empathetic provider isn't a nice-to-have. For someone chronically ill, it changes whether you actually follow through on a treatment plan, because you trust the person who gave it to you. [Stanford's Center for Compassion and Altruism Research and Education](http://ccare.stanford.edu/) has done real work on this. Medical training should teach empathy as a core clinical skill, not treat it as a personality some doctors happen to have.

**Chronically ill speakers face a barrier most conferences don't see**

Chronically ill and disabled speakers bring perspectives no one else in the room has, and they still run into unspoken discrimination getting booked. [This piece](https://inklusiv.ca/understanding-the-need-for-inclusion-as-public-speakers/) makes the case plainly: accessibility has to be a prerequisite for an event, not an afterthought bolted on when someone asks.

**Burnout in entrepreneurs isn't a mindset problem**

Resilience doesn't mean avoiding stress. It means having a way to metabolize it that doesn't cost you your health. [The APA has good material on this](https://www.apa.org/topics/resilience). Worth reading if you're building something and treating exhaustion as the price of admission.
