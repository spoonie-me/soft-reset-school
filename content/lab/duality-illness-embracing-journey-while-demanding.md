Chronic illness demands two contradictory things at once: acknowledge how real it is, and refuse to let it be the only thing you are. Society mostly pushes the second half without the first, treating "get over it" as advice instead of dismissal.

So there's a constant translation happening: telling the world "I'm fine" while your body is saying something else entirely, downplaying the very real need for support or a mobility aid because naming it feels like handing someone a reason to see you differently.

Acknowledging the illness isn't surrender. It's the precondition for actually advocating for yourself, because you can't ask for what you won't admit you need.

A few things that help hold both halves at once: using the forced downtime for something that's actually yours, reading, learning, a skill, rather than only recovery. Naming the condition out loud when silence would be easier, because silence is what keeps it invisible to the people who could otherwise accommodate it. And staying multidimensional on purpose: the diagnosis is real, and it isn't the whole story, and both of those stay true on the same day.

There's a middle path between defeatism and denial: acknowledging the hardship without surrendering authorship of your own story. That's not a permanent resolution. It's a daily rebalancing, some days easier than others, and the fact that it has to be redone constantly doesn't mean it isn't working.
